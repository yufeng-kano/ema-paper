# Billing — plans, credits and Kano Pay

Ema Paper sells a subscription that **includes the model spend**: the operator runs every parse and every chat turn on one Kano Proxy account of its own, and what a user may run is measured in **credits** (點數) granted per period. Users no longer connect an upstream or pick a model from a catalog; they pick a plan, and inside it a chat model from the few the operator offers. This doc is the contract for all of that: what a plan grants, how credits are priced and kept, how a subscription is bought and known, and the order in which the service moves there from the bring-your-own-upstream model of [product.md](./product.md). Decisions were taken on 2026-10-23; the numbers below are the operator's defaults, every one of them configuration.

## Plans

Two plans. Free is a platform policy, never a product at the payment provider; a paid plan is a row of `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` joined to a real price in Kano Pay's catalog.

| | Free | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Price | 0 | Kano Pay's catalog (never written into code or copy) |
| Credits per period | `FREE_CREDITS_PER_PERIOD` (150) | the plan's `credits` (5,000 in the launch configuration) |
| Period | the UTC calendar month | the subscription's paid period, as Pay reports it |
| Sandbox (`bash` in chat) | no | yes, the VM stops after 10 idle minutes (`SANDBOX_IDLE_SECONDS=600` on that deployment) |
| Workspace storage — documents and the files of any kind kept beside them, each counted once ([workspace.md](./workspace.md) § Files, § Quota) | `FREE_STORAGE_BYTES` (512 MB) | the plan's `storageBytes` (10 GB) |
| Parses at once | `FREE_MAX_JOBS` (1) | the plan's `maxJobs` (3) |
| Files | kept for as long as the account exists — **both** plans — save what the reader moves to the Trash, deleted for good 30 days later ([workspace.md](./workspace.md) § The Trash). Nothing is deleted when a period ends or a subscription lapses; a Free user over the storage cap can read and delete, not add ([workspace.md](./workspace.md) § Quota) |
| REST API and MCP | yes | yes |

- **Storage is set in decimal bytes.** The web app prints sizes 1000-based, as Finder does (`formatBytes`, [web.md](./web.md)), so a cap meant to read as a round number is one in decimal: `512000000` reads as *512 MB* and `10000000000` as *10 GB*, where `524288000` (500 MiB) would read as *524 MB* and `10737418240` (10 GiB) as *10.7 GB*. `WORKSPACE_QUOTA_BYTES` is only the ceiling a plan must fit under and no page prints it, so its default stays 10 GiB.
- **Every user has Free**, from the moment the `users` row exists, with no row of its own: a user with no active subscription *is* on Free. Empty Kano Pay configuration must not break Free.
- **Entitlements come from the plan the current period belongs to** (§ Periods): the storage cap, the concurrency cap and whether the chat agent gets `bash` are read from it wherever today's code reads `WORKSPACE_QUOTA_BYTES`, `MAX_JOBS_PER_USER` and `sandbox_configured`. A deployment with no runner (`SANDBOX_URL` empty) gives nobody `bash`, whatever the plan says.
- **Without a sandbox the chat agent runs as it does today without one** (`TOOLS_WITHOUT_BASH`, [chat.md](./chat.md) § Tools): page and figure views, the editor, skills, subagents, memory and translation stay; only `bash` goes. Which of those Free should keep is still the operator's open question; the contract here is the current set.
- A paid plan whose `planId` Pay does not know, or whose price is missing from the catalog, cannot be bought and grants nothing: `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` says what a plan *is* (credits, storage, sandbox, concurrency), the catalog says what it *costs*, and only the intersection is on sale ([kano-proxy-cloud `billing.md`](https://github.com/yufeng-kano/kano-proxy-cloud) follows the same join).
- **Top-up packs** (since 2026-10-26): a Pro user can buy more credits as a one-time purchase, a row of `KANO_PAY_PACKS_JSON` joined to a one-time product in Pay's catalog (§ Top-up packs). No third tier in this version.
- **Redeem codes** (since 2026-10-27): the operator hands out codes that grant credits — kept as a pack that was not bought — or days of a paid plan, banked and spent only while no subscription pays for the user (§ Redeem codes, § Periods *Granted days*). A gift, never a sale: Free and Pro alike redeem, nothing about them goes through Pay, nothing is refunded.

## Credits

**One credit is `CREDIT_USD` (US$0.002) of model cost at list price.** The rates below were set from production's own usage over the 60 days before 2026-10-23 (parse ≈ 5,150 input + 1,440 output tokens per page on `gpt-6-luna`, format and figure checks included; chat ≈ 115K input per turn, nine tenths of it prompt-cache reads, plus ≈ 3K output). The operator's Kano Proxy account runs on subscription pools, so list price is the pricing reference, not the operator's cost.

### A parse

- A job costs **`PARSE_CREDITS_PER_PAGE` (1) per page it parses** — the pages its `pages` spec selects, not the document's page count — whatever the model, since the operator picks it. That covers the figure check and the format check, which are not charged on their own.
- Charged when the job is **queued**, not when it finishes: the credits are reserved then, so a user without enough credits never queues a parse — the refusal names the pages' cost and the balance (`402 insufficient_credits`, § Refusals). A reservation becomes consumed when the job ends `succeeded` and is **released** when it ends `failed` — the user got nothing.
- **A dedup hit costs nothing** — no model ran ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Dedup) — and neither does a second upload of bytes already on their way (`reuse_in_flight`). **A retry reserves the job's credits again** ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Retrying failed pages): the job keeps its one entry, and the retry takes it back from the current period — dated then, and refused with `402 insufficient_credits` when the balance cannot cover it, nothing requeued — to be consumed or released by the retry's own end, as the first run's was. A document is charged once, by the run that delivers it, and a job that never succeeds costs nothing however often it is retried. A job queued before the ledger existed has no entry, and its retry reserves nothing, as its first run did. Until 2026-10-25 a retry reserved nothing, so a job that failed and then succeeded on a retry was never charged — a reader could make the format check fail on purpose, its prompt being theirs to write, and retry with the check off. **Re-converting** is a new job and is charged like one ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Re-converting a document), and so is **converting a file** kept in the library ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Converting a file) — keeping a file costs no credits, only storage. A parse on a page spec that is a subset of an earlier one is a new job too.
- The worker releases a reservation in the same transaction that marks the job `failed`, and consumes it in the one that marks it `succeeded`; a job requeued after a crash keeps its reservation. Nothing refunds a `succeeded` job.

### A chat turn

- A turn is charged **by the tokens its model calls really used**, every call of the turn together (subagent calls carry the turn's `turn_id` and count with it): `input × price.input + cache_read × price.cache_read + cache_write × price.cache_write + output × price.output`, prices per token from `MODEL_PRICES_JSON` (§ Models), summed in USD and **rounded up to whole credits once per turn**, never per call, with a floor of 1 credit. A `/translate` turn is a turn like any other.
- A turn needs a balance of **at least `CHAT_MIN_CREDITS` (1)** to be queued (`402` otherwise) — its cost is not known in advance. The runner settles the turn's entry **after every model call** (the entry's `amount` becomes the rounded-up total so far, the period's `spent` moves by the difference), and **when the balance is gone mid-turn** — the settled remaining balance is zero or below, a subagent's call included — **the turn still finishes, within a bound**: from then on at most `CHAT_OVERDRAFT_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` (25) more tool calls run, the turn's own and its subagents' together, each counted as it starts. A call past them answers an error result instead of running (a write already running finishes and commits as it would), and a subagent about to make another model call with none left stops there and reports what it has. Once they are used, the turn's next model call is its last: it goes with `tool_choice: {"type": "none"}`, so the model answers with what it has, and the turn ends `succeeded` when it does. Should that call still stop on a tool call, its tool calls are dropped, its text kept, and the turn ends with `turn_error` `out_of_credits`. Every call is charged as usual, nothing is refunded, and the period ends in the red by what that tail cost. That overdraft is not carried into the next period, and no screen shows a negative balance: an overdrawn period reads 0 left. The bound is separate from `CHAT_MAX_TOOL_CALLS_PER_TURN`, which still caps every turn: a turn that runs out of credits stops at whichever comes first. A turn the worker finishes on its own (`fail_turn`: a shutdown, a restart, an escaped error) closes the entry at what it was charged so far, like any other terminal state.
- The worker records every call's `usage_log` row as it does now ([chat.md](./chat.md)); credits are computed from those same token counts, so Usage and the ledger agree by construction.
- **A compaction turn is a turn like any other** ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Compaction): the reader's `/compact` needs the same minimum balance and is charged by its tokens, and the worker's idle compaction is enqueued only when that minimum is there — a conversation it cannot afford is left as it is, and its reader pays the cold re-read with their next message instead. What that re-read would cost is `recache_credits` in the conversation's payload: `context_tokens × price.cache_write`, rounded up to whole credits as a turn is ([api.md](./api.md) § Chat).
- **A web search is a flat price on top of the tokens.** Each `web_search` call the turn makes ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Tools) adds `WEB_SEARCH_CREDITS` (3) to the turn's amount — the Brave Search API's list price, US$5 per 1,000 calls, rounded up to whole credits with a margin — recorded as a `chat_tool_calls` row and settled in the same transaction, so the turn's entry carries it the moment the search is answered, whatever ends the turn after. A search Brave did not answer is not recorded and not charged. The settlement adds the searches to the token credits after the tokens' own rounding, so a turn of one search and a few hundred tokens costs 1 + 3. Once the balance is gone the tool is refused rather than run on the overdraft: the allowance after the credits run out is for finishing the answer, not for spending more. The other web tools — `search_papers`, `read_url`, `download_file` — cost no credits, only their tokens; their caps are counts, not money ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Tools). A call cut short — a Stop, the turn's timeout, a stream that broke — is recorded and charged for what it streamed ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Conversations, turns, events): stopping an answer does not make it free.

### Top-up packs

A **pack** is a one-time purchase of credits for a Pro user whose period is running out — the decision of 2026-10-26: one pack, bought as often as wanted, never a subscription. `KANO_PAY_PACKS_JSON` says what a pack *is* (`[{"packId": "credits", "credits": 2000, "validDays": 365}]`), Pay's catalog (`packs`, a one-time product each, `kano-pay/docs/integrating-apps.md`) what it *costs*, and only the intersection is on sale — as with the plans, no price is written into code or copy.

- **Pro only.** A pack can be bought only while the current period's plan is a paid one (§ Periods): `POST /api/billing/packs/checkout` answers **409** `plan_required` on Free, and no screen offers a pack to a Free user — the Free screens do not change. A pack bought on Pro is still spent on Free's month if the subscription lapses: the credits are the user's.
- **A pack is valid for `validDays` (365) from the day it was paid**, not for the period: `expires_at = paid_at + validDays`. It is not reset or topped up when the period rolls over, and what is left when it expires is gone.
- **Spending order.** A charge takes the period's grant first and the packs only once that is spent, the pack that expires first before the others (`expires_at`, then id). The balance a charge is measured against — `available` — is the period's remaining (0 when overdrawn) plus every active pack's remaining; `402 insufficient_credits` reports that sum as `remaining`, and so does `credits_remaining` in `GET /api/me`. A pack is **active** while its `status` is `paid` and it has not expired.
- **The chat overdraft is unchanged** (§ A chat turn): a turn's settlement moves its delta into the period first, then the packs, and only when both are gone does the period go negative and the `CHAT_OVERDRAFT_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` tail begin.
- **A release gives back where it took from**: a failed parse's reservation restores the period's share to the period and each pack's share to that pack, expired or not (an expired pack's credits are unusable either way), and a retry takes again from the period and the packs as they stand then.
- **Refunds.** The policy the public page states: a pack none of whose credits were spent is refunded in full on request within 14 days of the purchase; nothing once any were. The operator makes the refund at Dodo; Pay reports it as `purchase.updated` with `status: refunded`, and the app marks the row `refunded`, which reads as 0 left — whatever was spent from it stays spent and nothing is clawed back from the period.
- **Nothing is sold without Pay**: with Pay unconfigured or unreachable, Pay's checkout closed, or the pack unknown to either side, no pack is offered and a checkout for one is refused (§ Refusals).

### Redeem codes

A **redeem code** (兌換碼) is a code the operator hands out and a signed-in user types in — the decision of 2026-10-27: a gift, never a sale. A code grants one of two things, fixed when it is created: **credits**, kept as a top-up pack that was not bought (`credit_packs` with `source = code`, § Top-up packs), or **days of a paid plan**, banked and spent only on days no subscription pays for the user (§ Periods, *Granted days*). Free and Pro alike can redeem; Free still cannot buy a pack.

- **What a code is.** A `redeem_codes` row: `kind` (`credits` or `plan`), for credits `credits` and `valid_days`, for a plan `plan_id` — a plan of `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON`, the grants configured here, never a price, so a plan code works on a deployment with Pay unconfigured — and `days`; `max_uses` (1 is a personal gift, more a campaign code), `redeem_by` (optional: after it the code is dead), `label` (the operator's note, never shown to a user) and `disabled_at`. The code itself is `REDEEM_CODE_CHARS` (12) characters of a 32-letter alphabet without `0`, `O`, `1` and `I`, printed and typed as `<REDEEM_CODE_PREFIX>-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX`; what a user types is uppercased and stripped of everything but letters and digits, the prefix dropped, before it is looked up, so case, spaces and hyphens never matter. Codes are stored as printed, not hashed — the operator lists them — and never logged.
- **Who creates codes.** The operator, with `python -m sightread.codes` in the api container — `create`, `list`, `disable` ([self-hosting.md](./self-hosting.md) § Redeem codes); there is no page for it. `create` refuses a plan code whose `plan_id` is not configured, and prints each code once, with its prefix.
- **Redeeming** — `POST /api/billing/redeem {code}` (session + CSRF) — is one transaction under the user's row lock: the code row locked and checked — unknown or disabled → **404** `code_invalid`; `redeem_by` passed → **409** `code_expired`; `uses ≥ max_uses` → **409** `code_exhausted`; redeemed by this user before → **409** `code_redeemed` (one redemption per user per code, `UNIQUE (code_id, user_id)`) — then a `redemptions` row written, `uses` raised, and the grant made. A credits code adds a `credit_packs` row — `id = redeem-<redemption id>`, `pack_id = code`, `source = code`, its `credits`, `purchased_at = now`, `expires_at = now + valid_days`, `status = paid` — spent, released and retried exactly as a bought pack is (§ Top-up packs). A plan code adds `days` to the user's `plan_grants` bank and, when no paid period covers `now`, opens the granted period at once (§ Periods, *Granted days*), so the answer can say until when; a plan code of another plan than the one the bank already holds or runs is refused **409** `code_plan_conflict`. The answer: `{kind: "credits", credits, expires_at}` or `{kind: "plan", plan_id, days, days_banked, period_end}` — `period_end` null while a paid subscription runs and the days wait.
- **Attempts are limited.** Every refusal counts as a failure on the user's row (`users.redeem_failures`, `users.redeem_failed_at`): `REDEEM_MAX_FAILURES` (10) inside `REDEEM_FAILURE_WINDOW_SECONDS` (3600) answer **429** `rate_limit` with `Retry-After` until the window has passed; a success clears the count. The code space makes guessing hopeless either way (32¹² ≈ 10¹⁸); the cap is hygiene. The code typed is never written to a log, refused or not.
- **Granted credits are packs** in every rule of § Top-up packs — spent after the period's grant, by earliest expiry, given back and taken again with a release and a retry — with one difference: nothing refunds them, so `status` never leaves `paid`, and the webhook and the refresh never touch them (`source = code` rows are not Pay's; only Pay's count towards whether the refresh fetches purchases). The Usage section lists them beside the bought packs, each with its source.
- **Nothing is refunded or transferred**, and the public terms say so; a code once redeemed stays used — the `redemptions` row goes with the account, `uses` does not come down.

### Rates at the launch configuration

Per 1K tokens, and what the measured average turn comes to; the parse rate is the flat 1 credit per page (≈ 0.7 credits of list-price cost on `gpt-6-luna`).

| Model | input | cache read | cache write | output | ≈ per turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-6-luna` | 0.05 | 0.005 | 0.05 | 0.25 | 2 |
| `deepseek-v4.1-flash` | 0.15 | 0.003 | 0.15 | 0.6 | 4 |
| `gemini-3.8-flash` | 0.75 | 0.075 | 0.75 | 3.75 | 27 |
| `claude-sonnet-5-5` | 1 | 0.05 | 1.25 | 5 | 34 |
| `gpt-6-sol` | 1 | 0.1 | 1 | 5 | 36 |
| `gpt-5.6-terra` | 1 | 0.1 | 1 | 6 | 39 |
| `claude-opus-5-5` | 2 | 0.1 | 2.5 | 10 | 68 |

Those are `MODEL_PRICES_JSON` values divided by `CREDIT_USD`; the table is an illustration, the configuration is the truth. `gemini-3.8-flash` and `deepseek-v4.1-flash` (added 2026-10-27) are priced at their regular list rates — not Google's 2026 launch price, not DeepSeek's off-peak rate — and neither charges a cache write, so their cache-write rate is the input rate and is never reached ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Upstream). A web search is `WEB_SEARCH_CREDITS` (3) per call on top, the same on every model. OpenAI bills input past 272K tokens at twice the rate; the proxy's usage report does not say which tokens those were, so this service prices every token at the base rate and the operator carries the difference on the rare long turn.

### Periods

A user's **current period** is decided at every charge, from the subscription row and the clock:

1. A subscription whose `status` is `active` or `trialing`, whose `period_start ≤ now < period_end`, and whose `plan_id` is in `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` → the period is `[period_start, period_end)` on that plan.
2. Otherwise, **granted days** (§ Redeem codes): a `plan_grants` row whose open period holds `now` (`period_start ≤ now < period_end`) on a plan the deployment knows → that period on that plan; else a row with `days_banked > 0` on a plan the deployment knows → a new granted period is opened at `now`, of `min(days_banked, GRANT_PERIOD_DAYS)` days taken from the bank, its grant `ceil(plan.credits × days / GRANT_PERIOD_DAYS)` — the plan's full credits for a whole one.
3. Otherwise → Free: the period is the UTC calendar month containing `now`.

Each period has one `credit_periods` row per user, created on first use with `granted` = the plan's credits at that moment, and `spent` moving as entries are reserved, consumed and released. A period that was never used has no row and costs nothing. Rules that follow from this:

- **Credits do not carry over.** A new period is a new row with a fresh grant; whatever was left is left.
- **Upgrading mid-month** starts the paid period at Pay's `period_start` with the plan's full grant. The Free month's row stays as it is and is current again, remainder and all, if the paid period ends inside that month.
- **A plan change inside a period** (a later `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` edit, or Pay reporting another plan for the same period) raises the row's `granted` to the new plan's credits when that is higher and never lowers it: credits already shown as available are not taken back.
- **A lapsed subscription** (`past_due`, `canceled`, `paused`, or a period that ended) puts the user on Free's month at the next charge, with that month's row — which may already exist, from before the upgrade. `cancel_at_period_end` changes nothing until the period ends. Every charge re-reads the clock; nothing depends on a webhook arriving to expire access.
- **Reservations live where they were made.** A parse reserved in one period and finished in the next consumes or releases that period's row: the entry carries its `period_start`.
- **Packs are not periods.** A pack's row lives beside the period rows under its own expiry (§ Top-up packs); a period reset leaves it as it is, and a charge reads both.

**Granted days** (§ Redeem codes) are a bank on the user's `plan_grants` row — `plan_id`, `days_banked`, and the open granted period's `period_start` and `period_end` when one runs — and the rules above hold for them with these:

- **A paid subscription always comes first.** A granted period that a paid period starts inside is cut where the paid one begins: the whole days left of it — `floor((period_end − cut) / 1 day)`, `cut` the later of the granted period's start and the paid period's start — go back to the bank — written the moment the subscription is: the webhook or the refresh that stores it settles the period under the same lock (§ Kano Pay) — and the cut period's row keeps its grant, spent or not. When the subscription lapses the bank opens its next period at the next charge. So no granted day is spent under a paid one, a code redeemed on a paid plan waits, and every code adds to the one bank — they stack.
- **A granted period runs by the clock once it is open** — opened and not used, it runs out all the same — but a bank with no open period waits for the next use; a bank is never spent by time alone. A longer bank runs as consecutive periods of `GRANT_PERIOD_DAYS`, the last one shorter with its grant pro-rated.
- **A granted period is a period like any other**: its `credit_periods` row is created on first use with that grant, entries live in it, the overdraft is unchanged, and `period.source` says what the period is (`subscription`, `grant`, `free`). A bank whose `plan_id` is no longer in `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` is inert and the user is on Free until the plan is configured again.
- **Deciding the period writes only under the user's row lock.** A charge or a redemption, which hold it, persist the move — a granted period opened, one cut — reading the rows again under the lock so a bank another transaction just moved is not written over; the webhook and the refresh, which hold it to write Pay's subscription, persist the cut that subscription makes in the same transaction, so the days are banked whether or not a charge follows. A read (`GET /api/billing`, `GET /api/me`, an entitlement check) computes the same decision from the rows and writes nothing, so it takes no lock and holds none into the workspace's; the screen shows what the next charge will persist, and a period the bank opens is dated by the first charge or redemption that writes it.
- **Entitlements** — storage, parses at once, the sandbox — come from the granted plan as from a paid one. The portal is not offered without a Pay subscription; Pay's checkout is, and a reader on granted days who subscribes has their remaining days wait.

### Refusals

| Situation | Answer |
|---|---|
| A parse whose pages cost more than the current balance — the period's and the active packs' together (§ Top-up packs) | **402** `insufficient_credits`, body `{need, remaining, period_end}` — `remaining` that sum — the web app's upload confirmation and the API caller's hint. The upload is not stored and an upload ticket is not spent |
| A retry of a failed parse whose credits are more than the current balance | **402** `insufficient_credits`, same body; the retry is not queued |
| A chat send with a balance under `CHAT_MIN_CREDITS` | **402** `insufficient_credits`, same body (`need` is the minimum) |
| A turn whose balance runs out mid-way | the turn ends with `turn_error` `out_of_credits` (§ A chat turn) |
| A checkout for a plan not on sale, or while Pay says checkout is closed | **409** `checkout_unavailable` |
| A pack checkout for a pack not on sale, or while Pay says checkout is closed | **409** `checkout_unavailable` |
| A pack checkout by a user whose current period is Free | **409** `plan_required` (§ Top-up packs) |
| A redeem code that does not exist or is disabled | **404** `code_invalid` (§ Redeem codes) |
| A redeem code past its `redeem_by`, one used `max_uses` times, one this user redeemed before, or a plan code of another plan than the bank's | **409** `code_expired`, `code_exhausted`, `code_redeemed`, `code_plan_conflict` |
| Too many refused redeem attempts in the window | **429** `rate_limit` with `Retry-After` (§ Redeem codes) |
| Pay unreachable while a user asks for a checkout or the portal | **503** `pay_unavailable`; existing subscription rows are never changed by an outage |
| Deleting the account while a subscription still renews | **409** `subscription_active` — cancelled in the portal first (§ Kano Pay, *Deleting the account*); **503** `pay_unavailable` when Pay cannot be reached to say and the user has a `subscriptions` or `checkouts` row |

402 is new to the API; its envelope is the standard one ([api.md](./api.md) § Errors). No refusal costs the caller anything, and none of them ever consults the clock loosely: the balance is read under the user's row lock in the same transaction that writes the entry.

## The ledger

The tables, every one of them gone with the user ([database.md](./database.md)):

```text
subscriptions    user_id PK/FK, subscription_id UNIQUE, status, plan_id,
                 period_start, period_end, cancel_at_period_end, version, updated_at
checkouts        user_id PK/FK, plan_id, idempotency_key UNIQUE, transaction_id UNIQUE NULL,
                 created_at, completed_at NULL
pay_events       id PK (Pay's event id), type, user_id FK NULL, received_at
credit_periods   (user_id FK, period_start) PK, period_end, plan_id NULL, granted, spent,
                 created_at, updated_at
credit_entries   id PK, user_id FK, period_start, kind parse|chat, state reserved|consumed|released,
                 amount, job_id FK SET NULL NULL UNIQUE, turn_id FK SET NULL NULL UNIQUE, model NULL,
                 created_at, settled_at NULL
credit_packs     id PK (Pay's purchaseId), user_id FK, pack_id, credits, spent, status paid|refunded,
                 purchased_at, expires_at, version, created_at, updated_at
credit_pack_entries (entry_id FK credit_entries, pack_id FK credit_packs) PK, amount
pack_checkouts   user_id PK/FK, pack_id, idempotency_key UNIQUE, purchase_id UNIQUE NULL,
                 created_at, completed_at NULL
redeem_codes     id PK, code UNIQUE, kind credits|plan, credits NULL, valid_days NULL,
                 plan_id NULL, days NULL, max_uses, uses, redeem_by NULL, label,
                 disabled_at NULL, created_at
redemptions      id PK, code_id FK redeem_codes, user_id FK, kind, credits NULL, plan_id NULL,
                 days NULL, redeemed_at; UNIQUE (code_id, user_id)
plan_grants      user_id PK/FK, plan_id, days_banked, period_start NULL, period_end NULL,
                 updated_at
```

`credit_packs` also carries `source` (`pay` or `code`) and `redemption_id` (FK `redemptions`, SET NULL, NULL for a bought pack), and `users` carries `redeem_failures` and `redeem_failed_at` (§ Redeem codes).

- **`credit_periods.spent` is the balance's fast path and `credit_entries` its record**; the two agree because every write touches both in one transaction. `spent` counts the reserved and consumed entries of the period; a release subtracts its amount. `remaining = granted − spent` and may be negative only through a chat overdraft.
- **Reserving a parse** is one statement under the user's row lock (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on `users`, which `submit_parse` and the chat send already take for their caps): read or create the period row, then `UPDATE credit_periods SET spent = spent + :amount WHERE user_id = :u AND period_start = :p AND granted − spent ≥ :amount`; no row updated is the 402. The entry is inserted in the same transaction as the job, with `state = reserved`. A retry runs the same statement for the job's released entry, in the transaction that requeues the job, and moves the entry to the current period with `state = reserved` again; an entry in any other state is left as it is.
- **A pack is a `credit_packs` row written from Pay's canonical purchase** (§ Kano Pay): `credits` from the pack's configuration at the time it was paid, `spent` moving as charges take from it, `expires_at` fixed at `paid_at + validDays`. `credits − spent` is what it has left; it counts while `status` is `paid` and `expires_at > now`. A `credit_pack_entries` row says how much of an entry's amount each pack covered, so a release or a retry can move exactly that back (§ Top-up packs).
- **Reserving with packs**: under the user's row lock the period row and the active pack rows are read (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the period row, then on the packs, earliest expiry first) and `available` computed; under `need` is the 402. The period's share is taken by the conditional UPDATE as before, the rest from the packs in order, each by its own conditional UPDATE (`credits − spent ≥ share`), and the shares are recorded with the entry. A retry takes again the same way.
- **Settling a turn** (§ A chat turn) moves a positive difference into the period up to what it has left, then into the packs in order, then into the period past its grant — the overdraft; the pack shares are recorded as a reservation's are. The period row is locked (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) before its remaining is read, so two turns of one user settling at once read it one after the other: the second takes only what the first left of the period and spills the rest into the packs.
- **A redeemed code** writes under the user's row lock, after the code's own row: the `redemptions` row, then — for credits — a `credit_packs` row with `source = code`, which every rule above treats as a pack, or — for a plan — the `plan_grants` row and, through the same period decision a charge makes, the granted period it may open. The period decision writes `plan_grants` (a period opened or cut) under the user's row lock too — a charge, a redemption, the webhook and the refresh write it, a read never does — so two writers deciding it wait for each other (§ Periods, *Granted days*).
- **Lock order.** Every ledger write takes its row locks in one order — the user's row where the caller holds it, the job or the entry, the period row, then the active pack rows in expiry order, in one statement — on a reservation, a retry, a release and a settlement alike, and the webhook and the refresh lock the user's packs the same way before they write Pay's purchases (§ Kano Pay), so a charge, a settlement and a purchase written at once wait for each other rather than deadlock (PostgreSQL would abort one, and a settlement aborted in `_record_usage` would lose the usage row of a call already made).
- **Settling** is idempotent: the entry's `state` is the guard. A job finishing twice (a requeue that raced a finish) moves `reserved → consumed` once; a release of an entry already consumed does nothing and is logged. The chat entry is updated by its `turn_id`, and the period's `spent` by the difference between the new and the old `amount`, so a repeated settlement of the same usage moves nothing.
- **Deleting the account** ([auth.md](./auth.md) § Deleting the account) takes the ledger with it, as it takes `usage_log`. Nothing else deletes a row: the ledger is the billing record, kept as long as the account. Deleting a conversation takes its turns and sets the entries' `turn_id` to NULL, as it does for `usage_log`; a parse the retention deletes ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Retention) sets its entry's `job_id` to NULL the same way. Either way the amounts stay in the period, `spent` unchanged, and in the usage breakdown.
- **Periods are small** — one row per user per month or paid period — and are never swept. Entries are one per job and one per turn.

What the web app reads: `GET /api/billing` (§ Routes) for the plan, the period and its balance; the Usage tab's credits per day and per agent come from `credit_entries` grouped like `usage_log` is today ([web.md](./web.md) § Pages).

## Models and the operator's upstream

- **One Kano Proxy key for the whole deployment:** `KANO_PROXY_API_KEY`, in the server's `.env` ([deployment.md](./deployment.md)). The worker and the API present it on every upstream call. It replaced the per-user key the connect flow used to obtain; the flow, the stored keys and the provider connections went with it (§ Transition, step 4).
- **The parse model is fixed:** `PARSE_MODEL` (`gpt-6-luna`), one id, the same for every user; its bbox convention is resolved as today ([parsing.md](./parsing.md) § Coordinate conventions). The format check runs on the parse model unless `CHECK_MODEL` names another. The parse agent's fallback model, if the operator sets one, is `PARSE_FALLBACK_MODEL`; a user has no model settings for `parse` or `check` any more — their prompt, instructions and switches stay.
- **The chat models are the live catalog's priced ones, with no list of their own:** the models of the operator's Kano Proxy catalog (`GET {KANO_PROXY_BASE_URL}/openai/v1/models`, fetched live, no cache — on a group endpoint, exactly the group's model names) that `MODEL_PRICES_JSON` prices, **in `MODEL_PRICES_JSON`'s order**; the first is the default for a user who chose none. Kano Proxy decides what exists and the price table what is sold, so a model is added or withdrawn in one of those two places and nowhere else: a catalog model nobody priced is not offered, and a priced model the catalog does not list (a fallback target, a model the group dropped) is not either. `GET /api/agents/chat/models` answers that list. `agent_settings.model` for `chat` must be on it when it is saved (400 otherwise); a stored choice the list later loses is still shown as the current one and refused at enqueue, as a vanished model always was. (`CHAT_MODELS`, the comma-separated list this replaced, is ignored if still set.)
- **`MODEL_PRICES_JSON` must price every model the deployment runs on its own** — `PARSE_MODEL`, `CHECK_MODEL`, `PARSE_FALLBACK_MODEL` — and is what makes a catalog model a chat model, as `{"<model>": {"input": <USD per 1M>, "cache_read": …, "cache_write": …, "output": …, "context": <tokens, optional>}}` — `context` the model's context window, which the chat panel's ring measures a conversation against; a model without one is measured against `CHAT_CONTEXT_WINDOW_TOKENS` ([chat.md](./chat.md) § Compaction). The API and the worker **refuse to start** with an offered model unpriced: a model nobody priced must never run for free. A chat turn whose model has no price (the list changed under a queued turn) fails with `turn_error` `model_unpriced` before its first call, and a subagent whose own `model` has no price answers an error result before its first call instead of running — its calls would otherwise be charged as nothing.
- **Nothing in `MODEL_PRICES_JSON` is product data the UI shows as a price**: the web app shows credits per 1K tokens and per page, derived from it and `CREDIT_USD` by the API (`GET /api/billing`), never typed into the frontend.

## Kano Pay

Payment is through **Kano Pay** (`pay.yuufeng.com`), the operator's payment service shared with Kano Proxy, and the contract is the one its own docs define (`kano-pay/docs/integrating-apps.md`): an App registered in Pay's `APPS_JSON` with a service key, a return URL, a webhook URL and its plans; `POST /v1/checkouts`, `GET /v1/subscriptions/:subjectId`, `POST /v1/portal`, `GET /v1/catalog`; signed notifications. Pay is moving its own backend from Paddle to **Dodo Payments**, which is the merchant of record (tax, invoices, refunds, the customer portal); the App contract does not change with it, so nothing here names the provider. Ema Paper is the second App on Pay after Kano Proxy, whose adapter (`kano-proxy-cloud/docs/billing.md` § Kano Pay adapter) this one copies.

- **Subject id** is the `users.id` as a decimal string. It never changes for an account, so a subscription found at Pay for a subject is that user's.
- **Checkout.** `POST /api/billing/checkout {plan_id}` (session + CSRF) refuses a plan not on sale (§ Plans) and, with Pay's `checkoutEnabled` false, answers 409. It then takes the user's `checkouts` row under their row lock: a row for the same plan reuses its `idempotency_key`; another plan replaces the row with a fresh key. The key is written **before** Pay is called, so a retry after an ambiguous timeout repeats the same request and Pay returns the same checkout rather than a second one. Pay's `checkoutUrl` is validated (https, Pay's own host) and returned as `{url}`; the browser goes there. Nothing is granted from the return redirect: the user comes back to `/files?settings=billing` (the settings dialog's Usage section) and the page shows whatever `GET /api/billing` says, which is what the webhook or a refresh has written.
- **Pack checkout.** `POST /api/billing/packs/checkout {pack_id}` (session + CSRF) refuses a pack not on sale and Pay's closed checkout with 409 `checkout_unavailable`, and a user whose current period is Free with 409 `plan_required` (§ Top-up packs). It then takes the user's `pack_checkouts` row under their row lock as the plan checkout takes `checkouts`: the same pack's open row keeps its `idempotency_key`, another pack or a completed row gets a fresh key, written **before** Pay is called; `POST /v1/purchases` answers `{checkoutUrl, purchaseId, sessionId}`, the URL validated to Pay's origin and returned as `{url}`, the `purchaseId` kept on the row. The return lands on `/files?settings=billing` as a plan checkout's does, and the pack shows once the webhook or a refresh has written it.
- **Webhook.** `POST /api/billing/pay-webhook` carries no session: it verifies `Kano-Pay-Signature` (`ts=<unix seconds>;h1=<hex HMAC-SHA256>` over `<ts>.<raw body>` with `KANO_PAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, constant-time; a timestamp more than `KANO_PAY_SIGNATURE_TOLERANCE_SECONDS` off is refused), reads the body only for the event `id`, `type` and the subject, then **fetches the canonical subscription from Pay** (`GET /v1/subscriptions/:subjectId`) rather than trusting the body. In one transaction, under the user's row lock: insert the `pay_events` row (a duplicate id is a 200 and nothing else), upsert `subscriptions` **only when the fetched `version` is newer** than the stored one, settle the period the rows now decide — a granted period the subscription began inside is cut, its whole days banked (§ Periods, *Granted days*) — and mark the user's open `checkouts` row completed when the fetched subscription is paid and its `version` is later than the row's `created_at` — the purchase that checkout started. The subscription's plan must be in `KANO_PAY_PLANS_JSON` to grant anything; an unknown plan is stored with its id and grants Free. 2xx is answered only once the rows are committed. A body naming another App's id is refused and logged by id only. A subject that is not a user — an account deleted while its subscription still sends its ending events — is acknowledged (200 `{}`) and logged at info, with nothing fetched or recorded: Pay retries a refused delivery for ever.
- **Purchase notifications.** The same route takes Pay's `purchase.updated` (`{id, type, purchase: {appId, subjectId, purchaseId, …}}`): verified the same way, read for its id, type, App, subject and purchase id, then **the canonical purchases are fetched** (`GET /v1/purchases/:subjectId`, the subject's paid and refunded purchases) and written under the user's row lock with the event row. A `credit_packs` row is **created** for a purchase not yet known whose `packId` is in `KANO_PAY_PACKS_JSON` — credits and validity from that configuration, `purchased_at` Pay's `paidAt` — and an existing row's `status` follows Pay's only when Pay's `version` is newer than the stored one, never backwards; a pack the deployment does not configure is logged and skipped, and nothing lowers a stored pack's `credits`. The `pack_checkouts` row whose `purchase_id` the answer names is marked completed. 503 when the fetch fails or the purchase the event names is not in the answer yet, so Pay retries.
- **Refresh.** `GET /api/billing` reconciles with Pay at most once per `BILLING_REFRESH_SECONDS` (120) per user — the same fetch the webhook makes — so a missed notification is recovered on the next visit, the `checkouts` row completed and the period settled with it as the webhook would; a Pay outage leaves the stored row as it is and the response says `pay_reachable: false`. Nothing is reset or revoked because Pay could not be reached. The refresh also fetches the purchases — `GET /v1/purchases/:subjectId`, as the purchase notification does — when the user has an open pack checkout or a paid pack, so a missed `purchase.updated` is recovered the same way; a user with neither costs no second call.
- **Portal.** `POST /api/billing/portal` returns Pay's fresh `{url}`; it is never cached and never shown to a user without an active or lapsed subscription of their own — a user with no `subscriptions` row gets 404 `invalid_request`.
- **Refunds.** Nothing here refunds. A refund is asked for at the support address, decided by the operator and made by Dodo to the original payment method; the subscription it ends reaches the app as any change does — the webhook or the refresh — and the period's grant is not clawed back from the ledger. The policy a user is promised is the public page's (§ Transition, step 6). A refunded pack is the one refund that *is* recorded: Pay's `purchase.updated` marks the row `refunded`, which reads as 0 left (§ Top-up packs).
- **Deleting the account** ([auth.md](./auth.md) § Deleting the account) is refused while a subscription still renews — `status` not `canceled`, `expired` or `failed`, and `cancel_at_period_end` false — with **409** `subscription_active`. Pay has no cancel endpoint, and once the account is gone its subscription could be reached from the app no more: the portal opens only for a user with a `subscriptions` row, and the same Google account signing in again is a new subject. So the reader cancels in the portal first; a subscription set to end with its period deletes. The deletion reads the subscription fresh from Pay, as the webhook does, before it takes any lock, writes back what it reads under the user's lock — the newer version wins — and decides on the stored row; with Pay unreachable, a user with a `subscriptions` or `checkouts` row is answered 503 `pay_unavailable`, and with Pay unconfigured the stored row decides. A checkout the user opened that is not completed and younger than `KANO_PAY_CHECKOUT_LIFETIME_SECONDS` refuses as well, **409** `checkout_open`: paid after the account is gone, it would start a subscription no one could cancel from the app. An open `pack_checkouts` row refuses nothing: a pack paid after the account is gone renews nothing, and is refunded on request as any untouched pack is.
- **Errors.** Checkout and portal answer 503 `pay_unavailable` when Pay is unconfigured, unreachable, answers 5xx or answers something that does not validate (a checkout URL off Pay's origin, a portal URL that is not https); checkout answers 409 `checkout_unavailable` when the plan is not on sale, Pay's checkout is closed, or Pay refuses with a 4xx of its own (a subscription or an open checkout already stands). The webhook answers 401 `auth` for a bad or stale signature, 400 for a body it cannot read, 413 past 64 KB, and 503 when the canonical fetch fails or Pay has no subscription for the subject yet — Pay retries, and nothing is recorded. Every answer that is not 2xx leaves the rows untouched.
- **Catalog.** `GET /api/billing/catalog` is public, answers `{available, plans: [{plan_id, name, amount, currency, cycle, credits, storage_bytes, sandbox, max_jobs}], packs: [{pack_id, name, amount, currency, credits, valid_days}], free: {credits, storage_bytes, sandbox, max_jobs}, rates}` — the plans on sale, the packs on sale (`KANO_PAY_PACKS_JSON` joined to Pay's `packs`, § Top-up packs), the Free plan as this deployment configures it and the rates of `GET /api/billing` (§ Routes), so the pricing page needs no second request — cached in memory for `KANO_PAY_CATALOG_TTL_SECONDS` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=<that>`; with Pay unconfigured or unreachable it answers `available: false` with no plans, `free` and `rates` still, with `no-store`, never an error and never a number the operator did not configure at Pay. The public pricing page and the billing section both read it ([web.md](./web.md)).
- **Secrets:** `KANO_PAY_API_KEY` and `KANO_PAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET` live in `.env` only, are never logged, and their presence is the only thing the API ever says about them. No customer or payment payload is logged or stored beyond the fields above.

## Transition

The service runs today on each user's own Kano Proxy key. The move is made in steps, each a PR with its docs, each leaving the service runnable, in this order; the `docs/` of a step are rewritten by that step, and until a step lands the docs it will touch describe what runs, with a pointer here where the two will differ.

1. **This doc and the schema** — the five tables, empty, their ORM models, the configuration above with its validation, and the credit arithmetic as a pure module. No behaviour changes: nothing reads the tables yet.
2. **The ledger** — reservation at `submit_parse`, settlement in the worker's terminal commit, the chat runner's per-call settlement and `out_of_credits`, `GET /api/billing` and `/entries`, 402s, `credits` on jobs and turns. Every existing user is on Free from this step; a user who had connected their own Kano Proxy still runs on it (step 4 ends that), charged in credits all the same, and a chat model they chose must be priced in `MODEL_PRICES_JSON` or its turn fails `model_unpriced`. With Pay unconfigured, `GET /api/billing` answers `checkout_available: false` and `pay_reachable: null`.
3. **Kano Pay** — Pay's own switch to Dodo first (in the kano-pay repository: docs, adapter, test mode, one real purchase and refund), then Ema Paper's registration in `APPS_JSON`, the checkout, webhook, portal and catalog routes here (`billing/pay.py`, `routes/billing.py`), `subscriptions` written for real, and the plan's storage, concurrency and sandbox entitlements enforced from this step: `ledger.storage_limit`, `ledger.jobs_limit` and `ledger.sandbox_granted` replace the bare `WORKSPACE_QUOTA_BYTES`, `MAX_JOBS_PER_USER` and `sandbox_configured` reads, and the worker's claim applies the same per-user cap from the `subscriptions` row as it stands at claim time ([jobs.md](./jobs.md) § Concurrency caps). Existing users are on Free, so a deployment whose Free plan is smaller than the old ceilings tightens on deploy: a Free user over `FREE_STORAGE_BYTES` can read and delete, not add, and runs one parse at a time.
4. **The operator's upstream** — `KANO_PROXY_API_KEY`, `PARSE_MODEL`, `CHAT_MODELS`; the connect flow, `kano_proxy_keys`, `kano_connect_states`, `provider_connections`, `agent_settings.connection_id` and `agent_settings.fallback_model` removed with their routes and UI ([auth.md](./auth.md), [api.md](./api.md) § Upstreams, [agents.md](./agents.md)); the model dropdowns replaced by the offered list (`GET /api/agents/{agent}/models`: the parse agent's fixed models, the chat agent's `CHAT_MODELS` against the live catalog). A job queued on a user's own Kano Proxy key runs on the operator's — the same proxy — and a job queued on a provider connection fails when the worker takes it, as a job whose connection was deleted always did (`jobs.connection_id` stays as history, [database.md](./database.md) § Rules). A `parse` or `check` model a user had chosen is cleared by the migration; a `chat` model outside `CHAT_MODELS` stays stored, is shown as the current choice, and is refused at the next send.
5. **The web app** — the Billing section (now *Usage*, with the plan's storage and sandbox), the credits moments, the public pricing page.
6. **Public pages** — `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/refund`, prerendered in both locales, linked from the sign-in and pricing footers, with the support address and the operator's name read from `GET /api/site` (`SUPPORT_EMAIL`, `OPERATOR_NAME`; [web.md](./web.md) § Pages, [deployment.md](./deployment.md)). Dodo's site review needs them. The refund policy the page states — a first subscription refunded in full on request within 14 days of the purchase, a renewal not, a failed parse never charged, an outage credited or refunded pro rata — is the operator's; Dodo makes the refund to the original payment method and the app learns of it as it learns of any subscription change, from Pay (§ Kano Pay).
7. **Domain and DNS**, then **deploy**: the migration, the new `.env` variables, one monitored real purchase.

Existing users land on Free with that month's grant; a trial of Pro for them is the operator's call and would be a `subscriptions` row written by hand — nothing in the code invents one.

## Acceptance

Unit tests, stubbed HTTP, no Pay or model call ever ([testing.md](./testing.md)):

- Credit arithmetic: a parse's credits from a pages spec; a turn's from token counts and prices, rounded up once over the turn, floored at 1; an unpriced model refused; configuration validation (every offered model priced, plans well-formed, ceilings respected).
- Periods: Free's month and a paid period from a subscription row and a clock; a lapsed row falls back; a plan change raises and never lowers `granted`; a period row created once under concurrent first use (PostgreSQL).
- Web searches (PostgreSQL for the ledger parts): an answered search adds `WEB_SEARCH_CREDITS` to the turn's entry at once, settled through the period and the packs like tokens; a search Brave failed adds nothing; a search is refused once the balance is gone; the per-turn and per-day caps refuse with an error result; `rates.web_search_per_call` follows the configuration and is null without the key.
- The ledger (PostgreSQL): two parses racing for the last credits — one reserves, one gets 402; settlement idempotent (consume twice, release after consume); a release restores the balance; a chat overdraft ends the turn and is not carried over; a call stopped mid-stream is charged for the input it reported and the output that streamed; a retried parse reserves again, is refused 402 on a balance that cannot cover it, and is charged once, by the run that succeeds.
- The webhook: a wrong or stale signature refused; a duplicate event id answered 200 without a second write; a stale `version` ignored; an unknown plan grants Free; the canonical fetch is what is written, not the body; a subject with no account acknowledged with 200 and nothing written.
- Deleting the account: refused 409 while a subscription renews, allowed once it is set to end with its period, 503 with Pay unreachable and a subscription on record. A purged parse: its entry kept with `job_id` NULL, the period's `spent` unchanged.
- Checkout: the idempotency key stable across a retry; a plan not on sale refused; checkout closed refused.
- Packs (PostgreSQL for the ledger parts): a charge takes the period first and the packs by expiry, and is refused on the sum; a release restores each share where it came from, and a retry takes again; a turn's settlement spills from the period into the packs before the overdraft; an expired or refunded pack counts nothing; a pack checkout refused on Free (`plan_required`), for a pack not on sale, and with Pay's checkout closed, and its key stable across a retry; `purchase.updated` written from the canonical purchases, not its body — a pack created once with its configured credits and expiry, a refund applied once and never backwards, an unconfigured pack skipped, a purchase the answer lacks left for Pay's retry; the refresh recovering a missed purchase; `GET /api/billing`, `/catalog` and `/api/me` showing the packs and the availability; configuration validation of `KANO_PAY_PACKS_JSON`.
- Redeem codes: a credits code becomes a pack with `source = code` that is spent after the period and never refunded; a plan code on Free opens a granted period at once with the plan's entitlements and its pro-rated grant, on a paid plan banks the days, which open after the subscription lapses; a paid period starting inside a granted one cuts it and banks the whole days left; a longer bank runs as consecutive periods; every refusal (unknown, disabled, expired, exhausted, redeemed before, another plan) with its code, and the attempt cap; the CLI creates, lists and disables codes and refuses a plan not configured; the Usage payloads show the source, the bank and the redemptions.
- The migration: applies on a database holding existing users, jobs and usage rows, which it does not touch; the downgrade drops the five tables and nothing else.
